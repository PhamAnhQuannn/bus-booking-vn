# a street-name heuristic "rescued" 23 of 54 addresses against a 1–16-sample reference and 2 of 54 against a 5.408-sample one — the first number was a small-sample artifact I nearly published

Building `docs/Khach-San-Ho-Xuan-Huong.docx`, 65 of the in-band lodgings have a full address but no coordinate, so the 1 km filter cannot see them. Attempted rescue: a row whose street has **every** measured establishment inside the radius is itself inside. Against `luu_tru.json` alone (1–16 rows per street) it placed **23/54** and looked like a clean 42% coverage win. Widening the reference to `luu_tru` + `nha_hang` — **5.408 rows carrying both an address and a coordinate across 891 streets** — collapsed it to **2**: `Trần Phú` spans 203–15.082 m, `Phan Đình Phùng` 187–21.070 m, `Bùi Thị Xuân` 167–4.136 m. Đà Lạt streets run kilometres, so "all samples inside" with n=1 means *the sample is too small to have found the far end*, not *the street is short*. **Rule: a predicate of the form "all observations of X satisfy P" is meaningless without the count of observations — and the count must be a per-group minimum, not a corpus total. Before trusting a group-level "all inside" test, re-run it against the largest reference you can assemble and watch whether the answer moves; if it moves, the first answer was measuring your sample size. Greppable smell: `max(v) <= threshold` / `all(... for ... in group)` with no `len(group) >= n` guard beside it.** Encoded rather than remembered: `an_ngu_data.do_pho_khong_dinh_vi` takes `toi_thieu=3` and returns `it_mau` as its own bucket, and the document prints the four counts (2 inside · 55 straddling · 5 outside · 3 too-few-samples) so the negative conclusion arrives with the measurement attached instead of as a bare assertion. The 65 rows are published in their own section with address and phone and **no distance, real or estimated** — the lesson from the 207 price-less lodgings is that omitting them entirely is the worse error. Second finding in the same pass, same shape: I added a `so_nha_lech` flag for rows accepted on the distance axis whose house number is absent from Google's address, and it fired on **25/53 (47%)**. Spot-checking four before publishing: `Marguerite` "30-32 Nguyễn Chi Thanh" vs "30-32 Đ. Nguyễn Chí Thanh" was a **false positive** — `\bd\b` stripped the `Đ` but left the `.`, so the normalised strings differed by punctuation only; and `Nam Nguyên` was flagged against a **Plus Code** (`WCQQ+74P`), i.e. Google has no street address at all, which is *cannot compare*, not *disagrees* — the exact conflation logged on 28/07. Stripping punctuation first and requiring the **street name to appear on both sides** took 25 → **13**, all three re-checked samples genuine (`54`↔`48`, `80`↔`80B`, `03`↔`3f`). **Corollary: a newly-added flag firing on ~half the rows is a defect report about the flag until proven otherwise — sample the positives AND the negatives against ground truth before rendering it.** Also fixed here: the address normaliser ran on our side only, so a formatting difference in Google's string read as a content difference; both sides now go through one `chuan_dia_chi`, which also lifted resolution 51 → 54/67.
