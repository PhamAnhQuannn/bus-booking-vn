# I designed a ranking scale anchored to one hand-picked reference row, and my own portability test broke it: same 172 rows, different anchor → tier A went 12 → 27

Building a reusable eatery ranking for 63 provinces, I anchored everything on a benchmark establishment: floor `n ≥ max(n₀/100, 30)`, tier `CL = Wilson(R,n)/Wilson(R₀,n₀)`. It looked principled — the user names the standard, everything scales to it. The measurement that killed it took one loop: hold the 172 measured rows fixed and vary only the benchmark. `n₀=11.364 → 12` tier-A · `3.000 → 14` · `800 → 19` · `200 → 27`. A 125% swing in the headline number with **zero change to the underlying data**. Cause named by a review agent and confirmed: `n₀` is the **maximum** of a heavily right-skewed distribution (median 60, max 11.364 — a 189× spread), and the maximum is the least stable statistic available; letting it drive *both* the evidence floor *and* the score scale means the whole table pivots on one observation. Across 63 provinces "hạng A" would silently mean something different in every province. **Rule: a threshold or scale derived from a single extreme-valued row (max, top-1, "the famous one") is not a standard — it is a sample of size one, and it will not reproduce. Calibrate thresholds against the reference ONCE, then freeze them as absolute constants; never recompute the scale per dataset. Greppable smell: any scoring function taking a `moc`/`benchmark`/`reference` parameter that appears in the denominator, and any floor written as a fraction of another row's value.** Fixed by removing the benchmark from `xep_hang.xep()` entirely — it now takes `(R, n)` and nothing else, with `NGUONG = ((4.70,"A"),(4.40,"B"),(4.00,"C"))` and `SAN_TOI_THIEU = 30` as national constants; the user's chosen reference still sets the bar in effect (`Nậm Nướng` 4,9★/11.364 → W=4,888 → A) but no longer *is* the bar. **Two further corrections in the same pass, both mine, both found by writing the test before trusting the code.** (1) I diagnosed the wrong defect: I claimed `CL = W/W₀` compresses the quality axis. Measured at fixed n=2.000, the steps are ~0,065 per 0,3★ — essentially linear, no compression. The real defect is different and worse: at high `R`, the binomial variance `p(1−p)` collapses, so Wilson barely penalises small `n` — which is why `5,0★/167` scores **above** `4,9★/11.364`. That is why the evidence floor exists and must not be removed; Wilson alone does not close that case. **A plausible mechanism stated without measurement is a hypothesis, and I have now shipped one as a diagnosis twice.** (2) My own test asserted `"moc" not in xep.__code__.co_varnames` to prove the benchmark was gone — it failed, because `co_varnames` includes **loop variables** and the function body has `for moc, ten in NGUONG`. The test was inspecting locals while claiming to inspect the signature; correct form is `co_varnames[:co_argcount]`. Same shape as the 31/07 entry: **a failing self-written check is evidence about the pair (test, code), and twice now the test was the wrong half.** The other failing assertion was also mine — I expected `3,9★/221 → C` from the *previous* threshold set (4,5/4,0/3,5) after choosing 4,7/4,4/4,0. But tracing that one surfaced a genuine design hole worth more than the test: `hang=None` was carrying **two different meanings** — *below the evidence floor* and *above the floor but below every tier*. Split into `DUOI_CHUAN = "—"` (a conclusion) versus `None` (a gap), which is the 28/07 "could not test ≠ tested and failed" rule arriving at the tiering step.
